Provenance and Uncertainty in Synthesis

Exam taskPreserve information provenance and handle uncertainty in multi-source synthesis

Keep a claim-to-source mapping through every step, annotate conflicts instead of choosing a number, require dates so time differences are not mistaken for contradictions, and present each kind of content in its natural format.

On this pageWhat you need to knowIn the examCheck your understandingApply itSources

What you need to know

Attribution is lost in summarisation

Each time findings are summarised, it is tempting to drop the sources "to save space". By the final report, claims stand without citations. The fix is structural: every subagent outputs claim-source mappings, and every downstream step must preserve and merge them.

{"claim": "EU e-bike sales rose 12% in 2025",
 "source": "European Cycling Industry Report 2026", "url": "https://example.org/eci-2026",
 "excerpt": "sales increased by 12 percent year on year",
 "published": "2026-02-18", "method": "survey of 40 manufacturers"}

Conflicting figures: annotate, do not choose

Two credible sources give different market sizes: $12.4 billion (January 2026) and $8.7 billion (March 2025). Picking one silently, or averaging them, hides a real disagreement. Report both, with their sources, and let the reader or the coordinator decide.

Document analysis should finish with the conflicting values included and annotated; the coordinator decides how to reconcile them before synthesis.

Dates prevent false contradictions

Many apparent conflicts are time differences: a 2025 figure and a 2026 figure for a growing market both being correct. Require publication or data-collection dates in every structured output so synthesis can tell "contradiction" from "change over time".

Structure the report around certainty

  • Separate well-established findings (several independent sources) from contested ones.
  • Keep the original characterisation: if a source said "estimated" or "preliminary", say so.
  • Keep methodological context (survey size, sample, model) where it affects interpretation.

Use the right format for each content type

ContentFormat
Financial dataTables
News and eventsProse
Technical findingsStructured lists

Converting everything to uniform paragraphs makes figures hard to compare and technical detail hard to scan.

Decision rule
Every claim keeps its source and date

From the first subagent to the final report, no step may drop them.

Exam trap
Picking the most recent figure

"Use the newer number" sounds reasonable but discards a credible source without telling the reader. Annotate both.

In the exam

Scenario 3: reports with no citations, conflicting statistics, figures from different years, and uniform prose for mixed content.

Check your understanding

Question 1

Final reports state facts with no sources; attributions are lost as findings are summarised. What is the structural fix?

Show answer

Answer: C. Attribution must travel with each claim. Structured claim-source mappings, preserved through synthesis, stop it from being lost.

Question 2

Two credible sources report $12.4B (2026) and $8.7B (2025) for one market. How should the report handle it?

Show answer

Answer: D. Both figures are credible. Showing them with sources and dates lets readers see whether it is disagreement or change over time.

Question 3 · select 2

Which TWO should subagents include in every finding to support accurate synthesis? Select TWO.

Show answer

Answer: A, C. Source, excerpt and date let synthesis attribute claims and interpret time differences. Reasoning chains and self-scored confidence add volume without that value.

Apply it: Whitfield's provenance rules

Exercise · about 15 minCase: Whitfield Research Group

Whitfield's citations disappear and conflicting figures are quietly resolved. Write the provenance rules.

  1. Define the finding record every subagent returns.
  2. Write the conflict rule for document analysis and the coordinator.
  3. Lay out the report sections that separate established and contested findings.
  4. Choose formats for the market-size data, regulatory news and technical battery findings.
Check your work
Show model answer

1. Finding record. claim, source name, URL or document name, page or section, excerpt, published or collected date, method note, and which subagent produced it.

2. Conflict rule. Document analysis never resolves conflicts: it returns every value with its record and sets conflict: true on the group. The coordinator then checks dates (time difference or disagreement?) and method notes, and passes the annotated group to synthesis, which must show all values.

3. Sections. Summary; Established findings (two or more independent sources); Contested findings (each with the competing values and their sources); Coverage and gaps; Sources.

4. Formats. Market size: a table with value, year, source and method. Regulatory news: short dated prose. Battery findings: a structured list (chemistry, energy density, cost per kWh, source).
