    How to read a CCAR-P question

    Most items give a scenario, often with an artifact (an architecture, a prompt, a trace, an evaluation result, a decision record), and ask which constraint decides it and what trade-off follows. Six habits pick the right answer:

    1. Find the binding constraint. A legal obligation, a latency limit, an audit need or a budget outranks a preference, and usually rules out two options.
    2. Facts come from the system that owns them. Balances and status are fetched with a tool; numbers are computed in code. Claude does the language work.
    3. Enforce outside the model. Permissions, limits, idempotency and approvals live in code and gates, never only in the prompt.
    4. Start simple; change on evidence. The simplest pattern, model and prompt that meet the bar, changed only when an evaluation shows a gap.
    5. Measure the right thing. The slowest requests under load, cost per successful outcome, results per slice, several trials per case.
    6. Find the cause before the fix. Stop at the first wrong step in the trace. A bigger model is rarely the first answer.

    On exam day

    • 70 items in 120 minutes is about 1 minute 40 seconds each. Ten items are unscored and not marked, so treat every item as scored. Read how many responses to select before the options.
    • Every "Not" above is a real trap. If an option sounds safe but does not touch the cause, it is probably wrong.
    • The score is scaled to 1,000 with 720 to pass, so a raw percentage does not convert directly. Aim for 80% or more in the practice tests.
