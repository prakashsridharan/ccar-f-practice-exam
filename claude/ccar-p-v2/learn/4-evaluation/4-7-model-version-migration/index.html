    Model Version Migration

    Exam skillModel Version Migration

    Evaluating migration readiness from a model deprecation timeline and regression results for a candidate version, and selecting the response (pin, re-tune prompts, re-baseline evaluations, or defer) that manages behavioral drift within the timeline.

    Read a deprecation timeline and a candidate version's regression results, and choose the response (pin, re-tune prompts, re-baseline evaluations, or defer) that manages behavioural drift before the old version is retired.

    Written and maintained by Prakash Sridharan, Claude Certified Architect

    On this pageWhat you need to knowIn the examCheck your understandingApply itSources

    What you need to know

    Models are versioned, improved and eventually retired. A production system therefore changes model at least once in its life, and every change can shift behaviour. This skill is about managing that shift on a timeline you do not control.

    Pin, then migrate deliberately

    • Pin a dated model snapshot in production, not an alias that moves when a newer model is released. A pinned snapshot behaves the same until the team changes it, so every behaviour change can be tied to a decision.
    • Track deprecation notices. A retirement date is a deadline: after it, the snapshot is no longer served, and pinning cannot extend that.
    • Keep the evaluation suite ready, so a migration is a planned release rather than an emergency.

    The four responses

    ResponseWhen it fitsWhat it does not do
    PinYou need stability now and the retirement date is comfortably far awayIt does not avoid the migration; it only schedules it
    Re-tune promptsThe candidate regresses on some categories because it responds differently to the same promptIt does not excuse skipping the full evaluation afterwards
    Re-baseline evaluationsThe candidate changes what "good" looks like (format, length, refusals), so old baselines and judge calibrations no longer fitIt does not lower the floor to make a regression pass
    DeferThe candidate is not yet ready and the retirement date leaves time to fix itIt is not an option past the retirement date

    Read the regression results by category

    A candidate can score better on average and still fail a category the business depends on. Compare the candidate with the current baseline per category and against each floor, not only overall.

    Decision rule
    A floor is not renegotiated by the migration

    If the candidate falls below a category's agreed floor, the response is to fix the regression (re-tune prompts, adjust the design) and re-run the suite, not to lower the floor until the result passes.

    A migration plan

    1. Read the deprecation notice and put the retirement date in the plan.
    2. Run the full evaluation suite on the candidate and compare it with the baseline by category.
    3. Re-tune prompts for any category below its floor, and re-run.
    4. Re-baseline where expected outputs legitimately change, and recalibrate any model judge.
    5. Run the candidate in shadow on a sample of live traffic, then canary a small share of real traffic.
    6. Switch, keep the old snapshot available until the retirement date as a rollback, and record the change.
    Example
    Better on average, worse where it mattered

    An insurer's claims assistant was pinned to a snapshot due for retirement in 60 days. The candidate scored 2 points higher overall, but field extraction fell from 94% to 88% against a floor of 90%. The team stayed pinned, re-tuned the extraction prompt with examples for the new model, re-ran the suite, and switched after three weeks with every category above its floor.

    Exam trap
    Switching because the average went up

    An overall gain hides category regressions. The release decision is made category by category.

    Exam trap
    Release notes instead of evaluation

    A note that a new model is compatible with the previous one is not evidence about your prompts, your data and your floors.

    In the exam

    Migration items give a deprecation timeline and regression results for a candidate version, and ask which response manages behavioural drift within the timeline. Check the dates first (deferring past retirement is impossible), then the results by category against their floors. Pin for stability while you work, re-tune prompts for regressions, re-baseline when expected outputs legitimately change, and defer only when time allows. Distractors switch on the average, lower a floor to pass, rely on aliases, or treat release notes as evidence.

    Check your understanding

    Question 1

    A pinned snapshot retires in 30 days. The candidate passes every category except one, which is 2 points below its floor. What is the best response?

    Show answer

    Answer: C. The deadline is fixed and the floor stands. Fixing the regression and re-running fits the timeline; deferring past retirement is not possible.

    Question 2

    Why should production pin a dated snapshot rather than an alias?

    Show answer

    Answer: A. A pinned snapshot keeps behaviour stable and every change deliberate. It does not stop retirement, and a later migration may still need prompt work.

    Question 3

    A candidate model writes shorter, more direct answers, and the model judge calibrated on the old model now scores them lower even though reviewers prefer them. Which response fits?

    Show answer

    Answer: D. The expected outputs legitimately changed, so the baseline and the judge's calibration no longer fit. Forcing the old style or skipping evaluation ignores what the reviewers show.

    Apply it: Plan Prairie State's extraction migration

    Exercise · about 20 minCase: Prairie State Mutual

    Prairie State's intake extraction runs on a pinned snapshot that will be retired in 75 days. On the evaluation suite the candidate version scores 93% overall (the current snapshot scores 91%). By category: printed forms improve, handwritten mail forms fall from 89% to 84% against a floor of 87%, and medical-report fields stay level. The acknowledgement drafts from the candidate are shorter, and the model judge, calibrated on the current snapshot, scores them lower.

    1. Decide the response for each category and for the drafts.
    2. Write the migration plan with dates counted back from the retirement date.
      Done when: the plan switches before retirement with every floor met, and has a rollback.
    3. List what is recorded for the change.
    Check your work
    Show model answer

    1. Responses.

    AreaResponse
    Printed forms, medical reportsNo change needed; carry the baseline forward
    Handwritten mail formsRe-tune: add examples of handwritten forms to the extraction prompt, re-run until at or above 87%
    Acknowledgement draftsRe-baseline: have reviewers label 100 candidate drafts, recalibrate the judge, then judge the candidate
    ProductionStay pinned to the current snapshot until the switch

    2. Plan. Day 75 (now): read the notice, freeze the baseline. Days 75 to 55: re-tune the handwritten-form prompt and re-run the suite. Days 55 to 45: re-baseline and recalibrate the judge. Days 45 to 30: shadow run on 10% of live claims, comparing extracted fields with the current snapshot. Days 30 to 20: canary at 10% of traffic. Day 20: switch, keeping the current snapshot as the rollback until it retires.

    3. Record. The old and new snapshot identifiers, suite results by category before and after, the prompt changes, the judge recalibration, the shadow and canary results, the switch date and the rollback condition.
