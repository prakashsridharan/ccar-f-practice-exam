    Adapting Output and Choosing the Format

    Exam skillOutput Adaptation and Format Selection

    Applying editing, adaptation, and output-format selection to a raw Claude output for its audience or delivery channel, including inline responses, artifacts, structured data, code-executed results for computation that must be exact, and Skill-produced documents.

    Claude drafts; you deliver. Edit for clarity, tone and the reader, choose the format by how the output will be used, and compute any number that must be exact instead of letting it be written as prose.

    Written and maintained by Prakash Sridharan, Claude Certified Architect

    On this pageWhat you need to knowIn the examCheck your understandingApply itSources

    What you need to know

    An accurate draft is not yet a deliverable. The gap is the editing pass, where you apply your knowledge of the audience and the channel.

    Adapt for the reader

    AudienceLead withKeepLeave out
    ExecutivesThe decision and its impactA few key numbersMethod detail
    Working teamWhat to do, who owns itDetail and evidenceExecutive framing
    External or publicWhat they need to know or doPlain languageInternal detail and anything that identifies individuals

    The facts stay the same; selection, depth and tone change. Adapting for an external audience also decides what is disclosed: small-area counts or quotes from a confidential source can identify people.

    Choose the format by use and reliability

    FormatUse it for
    Inline replyQuick answers acted on in the chat
    ArtifactSubstantial, self-contained content you will edit or reuse, including small interactive tools
    Structured data (tables, fixed columns)Data people will sort or load into another tool
    Code-executed resultAny number that must be exact: totals, rates, margins
    File creation or a SkillReal Word, Excel, PowerPoint or PDF files, especially in a fixed template
    Decision rule
    Compute, do not write, numbers that matter

    A total written in prose is a plausible guess. Code execution runs the calculation on the actual data and makes it checkable. Claude still writes the code, so read the logic (for example, how "retained customer" was defined) or spot-check a few rows.

    Compare before you edit

    When quality matters, generate two or three drafts and edit the strongest, instead of rescuing the first one. The final version still needs your review.

    Exam trap
    One document for everyone

    Sending the same full draft to executives and the working team serves neither. A 14-finding analysis for a 10-minute meeting needs the three findings that matter most, with the full set in a reference document.

    In the exam

    Items give a raw output plus its audience or channel and ask for the best adaptation or format. Match the format to use (scan on a phone, load into a spreadsheet, edit over several rounds, present to a board) and use code execution for exact numbers. Distractors keep jargon, send one version to all audiences, or rely on prose arithmetic.

    Check your understanding

    Question 1

    An analyst needs the exact total of a 4,000-row expense file. Which output is most trustworthy?

    Show answer

    Answer: C. Exact figures should be computed, not generated in prose.

    Question 2

    A charter will go through several rounds of edits with a sponsor. Which format fits?

    Show answer

    Answer: A. Substantial documents that will be edited belong in an artifact.

    Question 3

    A 1,100-word memo with the key change in paragraph six will be read on phones. Which edit helps most?

    Show answer

    Answer: D. Readers on phones need what changed first, in short, scannable text.

    Apply it: Adapt Ironwood's weekly report

    Exercise · about 15 minCase: Ironwood Logistics

    Claude produced one long weekly analysis: on-time shipments fell from 95% to 89% at two sites because of a carrier change and new hires; overtime rose 14%. The VP Operations has five minutes; the two site managers must act this week. Figures came from Claude's prose reading of the spreadsheets.

    1. Fix the reliability problem first.
    2. Write the VP version (three to five lines).
    3. Outline the site-manager version.
      Done when: each version fits its reader and the numbers are computed.
    Check your work
    Show model answer

    1. Reliability. Recompute on-time rates and overtime with code execution on the Monday spreadsheets; check the two sites' figures against the raw rows before anything is sent.

    2. VP version. "On-time shipments fell to 89% (target 95%) at Joplin and Wichita this week. Cause: the switch to a new regional carrier plus four new pickers still in training. Overtime is up 14% as a result. Ask: approve one week of the previous carrier on the Joplin route while the new one is retrained."

    3. Site-manager version. Per site: computed on-time rate by day and route, the late loads with carrier and cause, the training schedule for new pickers with owner and dates, and the overtime by shift, with actions due Friday.
