    Is This Output Fit for Purpose?

    Exam skillOutput Evaluation and Fitness for Purpose

    Evaluating a Claude output against its requirements, source material, or professional standards, including accuracy, completeness, relevance, fitness for purpose, and classification as ready to use, requiring revision, or requiring human correction.

    Judge every output against three references (the requirements, the sources and the professional standard) and check completeness separately from accuracy. Then classify it as ready to use, needs revision or needs human correction.

    Written and maintained by Prakash Sridharan, Claude Certified Architect

    On this pageWhat you need to knowIn the examCheck your understandingApply itSources

    What you need to know

    Output that reads well is not the same as output that is fit for purpose. The AI Fluency framework calls the skill of judging output Discernment. It is a check against fixed references, run the same way every time, not a feeling about whether a draft looks good.

    Three references

    ReferenceThe questionExample failure
    RequirementsDoes it do everything that was asked, and nothing that was not?A proofread that also rewrote two contract clauses
    SourcesDo claims match the documents supplied?"$12 per unit" without "minimum 5,000 units"
    Professional standardsWould it pass in this field and for this audience?An accurate letter that ignores a bilingual requirement

    Accuracy and completeness fail separately

    Accuracy asks whether what is there is right. Completeness asks whether anything that matters is missing. A security summary can be correct on every point and still omit breach notification, the reader's main concern. Missing things are harder to see than wrong ones, so check against what the reader needs, deliberately.

    Example
    Precision is not completeness

    Claude flagged four invoice mismatches, and all four were real. That proves nothing about the 56 invoices it passed. Sampling the unflagged ones is the only way to find misses.

    Three verdicts

    VerdictWhenNext step
    Ready to useMeets requirements, matches sources, clears the standard for its useNormal review, then use
    Needs revisionClose, with a specific gapName the gap, re-prompt or edit, re-check
    Needs human correctionStakes, errors or uncertainty mean the draft cannot standA qualified person takes over; the draft can still guide them

    Calibrate to the use. An internal brainstorm with a few generic ideas is ready as a discussion starter. A regulatory gap analysis done without the regulation text needs a specialist, however confident it sounds.

    Exam trap
    Classifying by appearance

    Polished formatting, confident tone and correct spelling say nothing about fitness. Equally, a blanket rule such as "AI drafts always need three rounds" ignores the evaluation.

    In the exam

    Items give an output, its purpose and the sources, and ask for the verdict or the missing check. Look for the specific gap: an omitted condition, a missing reader concern, a change beyond the request, an audience mismatch. Distractors accept the draft because the numbers match, discard it entirely, or check only formatting.

    Check your understanding

    Question 1

    A summary of three supplier quotes is accurate but drops one supplier's minimum order. The team will choose a supplier from it. Verdict?

    Show answer

    Answer: B. A specific, fixable gap that affects the decision calls for revision.

    Question 2

    Which check finds the problem when a report is accurate but leaves out the reader's top concern?

    Show answer

    Answer: C. Completeness is a separate check from accuracy.

    Question 3

    Ten internal campaign ideas include a few generic ones; they will open a team discussion. Verdict?

    Show answer

    Answer: A. Low-stakes internal use sets a light standard.

    Apply it: Classify Copperline's drafts

    Exercise · about 15 minCase: Copperline Credit Union

    Member services has three Claude drafts: (A) a reply explaining the overdraft fee policy to a member, accurate but stiff, for a member who mentioned a death in the family; (B) a board summary of a new regulation, written without the regulation attached; (C) an internal list of ideas for reducing branch wait times.

    1. Run the three references for each draft.
    2. Give each a verdict and the reason.
    3. State the next step for each.
      Done when: every verdict names a specific reference.
    Check your work
    Show model answer

    A. Needs revision. Requirements and sources pass (the fee policy is correct), but the professional standard for a grieving member fails on tone. Next step: the agent rewrites the reply with empathy and checks the policy details against the member's account before sending.

    B. Needs human correction. The source check cannot be done because the regulation was never supplied, and the stakes are regulatory. Next step: the compliance team uses the draft as a starting list, checks every point against the regulation text and owns the board summary.

    C. Ready to use as an internal discussion starter. No sources to check, low stakes, easily revised. Next step: share with the branch managers' meeting.
