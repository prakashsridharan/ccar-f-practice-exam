    Connectors and Knowledge Sources

    Exam skillConnector and Knowledge Management

    Applying connector and knowledge management to the sources a workflow needs, including connecting sources such as Google Drive and Gmail, upload-versus-connector trade-offs, curating uploads, and keeping connected knowledge current, relevant, and free of noise.

    Give a workflow the sources it needs and no more. Connect live sources that change often, upload stable or point-in-time material, curate uploads to one current version, and keep connected knowledge scoped, current and free of noise.

    Written and maintained by Prakash Sridharan, Claude Certified Architect

    On this pageWhat you need to knowIn the examCheck your understandingApply itSources

    What you need to know

    What Claude knows in a workflow comes from two places: files you upload and sources you connect. Choosing well, and keeping both clean, decides whether answers stay current and relevant.

    Upload or connect?

    UploadConnector
    What it isA point-in-time copyLive access to a source you can already reach
    Best forStable material (a brand guide), or analysis that must be tied to one frozen versionMaterial that changes (a weekly catalog, today's email, the calendar)
    RiskGoes stale silently when the source changesToo broad a connection adds noise and exposure
    Example
    Two sources, two choices

    A product catalog updated weekly in Drive: connect it. A brand guide updated once a year: upload it to the Project and replace it when it changes.

    Connector boundaries

    • Connectors work within your own permissions. Claude cannot reach a shared mailbox you cannot open.
    • Each connector has defined actions. Some can draft or send only with your approval, and admins can restrict actions, for example search allowed and sending blocked.
    • On Team and Enterprise plans, admins decide which connectors are enabled and for whom.
    • A connector may return only part of a file; a Drive read may extract the text without processing embedded images.

    Learn a connector's boundary before building a workflow on it, so a limit is not mistaken for a bug.

    Curate the knowledge

    • One current version of each document; remove superseded versions when you add new ones.
    • De-duplicate: two copies of the same interview make its themes look twice as common.
    • Label inputs so their role is clear ("approved policy," "draft responses").
    • Prune what is unrelated to the Project's purpose; noise in is noise out.
    • Scope connections to the folders the workflow needs, such as executed contracts, not all drafts.
    Exam trap
    Connect everything, just in case

    Connecting the whole company drive brings in drafts, duplicates and data the task should not touch. Least privilege applies to knowledge too.

    In the exam

    Items describe stale answers, conflicting versions, a connector that "does not work," or a choice between uploading and connecting. Match the source to how often it changes, scope connections, curate to one current version, and recognize permission and capability boundaries. Distractors connect everything, keep every version, re-upload drafts, or switch models to fix conflicting sources.

    Check your understanding

    Question 1

    A pricing sheet uploaded in January is updated monthly in Drive. What is the main risk?

    Show answer

    Answer: A. Uploads are static copies and go stale.

    Question 2

    Claude finds Gmail threads the manager can see, but not a shared mailbox the manager cannot access. Why?

    Show answer

    Answer: C. Claude sees only what the user can access.

    Question 3

    Answers about travel rules conflict; knowledge holds three versions of the policy. Fix?

    Show answer

    Answer: D. Conflicting versions cause conflicting answers.

    Apply it: Source Brightwater's client reports

    Exercise · about 15 minCase: Brightwater Communications

    Analysts build monthly client reports from ad-platform exports (new each month, in Google Sheets), the client's brand guide (changes yearly), last year's annual strategy (fixed), and a shared Drive folder that also holds every client's drafts.

    1. Decide upload or connect for each source.
    2. Scope the connection.
    3. Set a curation routine.
      Done when: each source is current and no other client's material is reachable.
    Check your work
    Show model answer

    1. Monthly exports: connect through Google Drive (current each month). Brand guide: upload to the client's Project and replace when it changes. Annual strategy: upload (fixed reference).

    2. Connect only the client's own report folder, not the shared drive with every client's drafts; the competing grocery clients each have their own folder and their own Project.

    3. At each month end, the Analytics Lead removes superseded uploads, checks there is one version of each document, and confirms the connected folder contains only that client's files.
