    From Question to Actionable Plan

    Exam skillEnd-to-End Deliverable Workflows

    Applying Claude across the lifecycle of a research, analysis, or planning deliverable, including requirements analysis, research with cited sources, data analysis through code execution, and synthesis into an actionable plan.

    Use Claude across the whole life of a research, analysis or planning deliverable. Turn a vague ask into requirements, gather cited research, compute the numbers, and synthesize an actionable plan, while people keep the judgment calls Claude cannot see.

    Written and maintained by Prakash Sridharan, Claude Certified Architect

    On this pageWhat you need to knowIn the examCheck your understandingApply itSources

    What you need to know

    A deliverable such as a business case, a vendor recommendation or a staffing plan has a lifecycle. Claude can help at every stage, but each stage needs the right method and a check before the next one builds on it.

    The four stages

    StageClaude's roleMethodCheck
    1. RequirementsTurn a vague ask ("grow our impact") into goals, scope, constraints and success measures; flag what is ambiguous or missingStructured analysis of the brief and inputsThe owner agrees the requirements
    2. ResearchGather external evidenceResearch or web search with citationsOpen and check the key sources
    3. AnalysisCompute from internal dataCode execution on uploaded filesSpot-check figures and logic
    4. SynthesisDraft the plan or recommendationBuilt only from checked inputs, each claim traceableThe owner judges feasibility and decides
    Decision rule
    Plans rest on computed numbers and sourced claims

    A staffing plan built on a guessed utilization rate is a guess. Built on code-executed analysis of real data, with assumptions stated, it can be defended line by line.

    Make the plan actionable

    An actionable plan gives each action a priority, owner, date and measure. Recommendations should link to the evidence behind them (interview quotes, computed figures), so reviewers can check the reasoning.

    What stays with people

    Claude cannot see budget limits, a hiring freeze, two departments' history or a client's risk appetite unless told, and some of it should not be delegated anyway. Supply known constraints as context, and keep the final decision with the accountable owner.

    Exam trap
    Filling gaps with assumptions

    When an input is missing (a market growth estimate, a constraint), Claude fills it with something plausible, such as 15% growth instead of your 8%. State inputs and assumptions explicitly.

    In the exam

    Items describe a planning or analysis deliverable and ask for the best overall workflow, the missing step, or what to fix before the plan goes to decision makers. Prefer: requirements first, cited and checked research, computed numbers, traceable synthesis, human decision. Distractors ask for the recommendation in one prompt, use recalled "typical" figures, keep unsourced statistics, or let Claude finalize and send.

    Check your understanding

    Question 1

    A plan for the city council cites "a 23% drop in delays in comparable cities," with no traceable source. What must happen?

    Show answer

    Answer: D. Every claim in a plan for decision makers must trace to a checked source.

    Question 2

    Which step in an analysis workflow should usually stay with a person?

    Show answer

    Answer: B. Judgment that depends on context Claude cannot see stays human.

    Question 3

    A churn plan lists 12 actions with no owners or dates. What makes it actionable?

    Show answer

    Answer: D. An actionable plan says who does what by when.

    Apply it: Plan Ironwood's warehouse expansion study

    Exercise · about 25 minCase: Ironwood Logistics

    Leadership asks the PMO: "Should we add a twelfth warehouse, and where?" Inputs: three years of shipment volumes by ZIP code (spreadsheets), lease quotes for three candidate sites, published regional freight forecasts, and a capital budget cap the CFO has not shared with Claude.

    1. Write the requirements you would agree first.
    2. Lay out the research and analysis steps with methods.
    3. Describe the synthesis and what stays with people.
      Done when: every number is computed or sourced and the decision owner is clear.
    Check your work
    Show model answer

    1. Requirements (agreed with Grace Kim and the VP Operations). Decision: build, lease or neither in the next 18 months. Criteria: delivery time to customers, cost per shipment, lease cost, growth capacity. Constraint: capital cap (to be supplied). Output: a ranked comparison of the three sites plus a "no expansion" option, with assumptions.

    2. Research and analysis.

    • Research for regional freight forecasts; the team opens and checks the cited sources and dates.
    • Code execution on shipment volumes by ZIP code: demand centers, growth trend, distance from each candidate site.
    • Code execution on lease quotes and shipment volumes: cost per shipment by option.
    • Checkpoint: spot-check volumes and distances for one site against the raw data.

    3. Synthesis. Claude drafts the comparison and a recommendation with each figure linked to its source or calculation, and the assumptions listed. The PMO adds the capital cap and known constraints; leadership makes the decision.
