    Building a Prompt That Fits the Job

    Exam skillPrompt Construction

    Applying prompt structure to a business objective, its audience, constraints, and source material, including role, context, task, output format, and the context Claude does not have by default, to produce a first response that is fit for purpose.

    A first response is only as good as the specification behind it. Give Claude the role, the context it cannot know, one clear task, the constraints and the output format, scaled to what the job needs.

    Written and maintained by Prakash Sridharan, Claude Certified Architect

    On this pageWhat you need to knowIn the examCheck your understandingApply itSources

    What you need to know

    Most weak first responses are not model failures. They are specification failures: the request left out something only the person asking knew. The AI Fluency framework calls the skill of specifying work Description.

    The five components

    ComponentWhat it controlsTypical gap
    RoleVocabulary, depth and point of viewRarely the main problem on its own
    ContextAudience, situation, prior decisions, source materialThe most often missing component
    TaskThe one action wanted: summarize, compare, draft, identifyA vague verb such as "review" or "look at"
    ConstraintsLength, tone, what to include or avoid, house rulesOutput the wrong length or tone, or invented terms
    Output formatTable, bullets, memo, email, fixed headingsOutputs that cannot be compared or reused

    Not every request needs all five. A quick question needs a task. A client deliverable needs all five.

    Context Claude does not have by default

    Claude cannot see your inbox, org chart, last meeting, pricing rules or the decision a document will support, unless you supply it or connect a source. When context is missing, Claude fills the gap with something plausible: a generic paragraph, a typical policy, an invented incentive.

    Example
    The same data, two requests

    "Summarize our Q3 results" returns three paragraphs that could describe any company. "Summarize the attached Q3 data for our regional director, who cares about cost and throughput. Cover only metrics that moved more than 10% against target, as a headline and three one-sentence bullets" returns a draft that needs two minutes of editing.

    Grounding and permission to say "not covered"

    For factual work, add two lines: answer only from the material provided, and say when it does not cover something. This turns open-ended writing into bounded use of your sources, and it is the cheapest defense against invented facts.

    Examples beat adjectives

    When a style is hard to describe ("casual and question-led"), one or two short examples show it more precisely than adjectives do.

    Exam trap
    Roles and adjectives do not add facts

    "You are a world-class expert" and "make it more persuasive" feel like improvements, but they give Claude no facts. If the draft is generic or wrong, look for the missing context first.

    In the exam

    Items describe a disappointing first response and ask what to add. Find the component the output points to: generic output means missing context; the wrong action means an unclear task; the wrong length or tone means a missing constraint; outputs that cannot be compared mean no fixed format. Distractors offer a role, a bigger model, more drafts or adjectives.

    Check your understanding

    Question 1

    A draft renewal email invents a 10% loyalty discount. What prevents this most directly?

    Show answer

    Answer: C. Claude added a plausible incentive because nothing ruled it out; a constraint on the terms closes the gap.

    Question 2

    Six candidate summaries will be compared side by side by a panel. Which component matters most?

    Show answer

    Answer: A. Comparability depends on identical structure; length and role do not provide it.

    Question 3

    A summary of a contract is accurate but stresses the wrong clauses for tomorrow's exit decision. What was missing?

    Show answer

    Answer: C. Without knowing the decision, Claude mirrors the document's own proportions.

    Apply it: Specify Brightwater's campaign brief

    Exercise · about 15 minCase: Brightwater Communications

    An account executive types: "Write social posts for the spring sale." The client is the smaller of the two grocery chains. The posts must use the client's name exactly as "FreshWay Market," never mention competitors, stay under 200 characters and drive sign-ups for the loyalty app.

    1. List what Claude cannot know from the one-line request.
    2. Rewrite the request with all five components.
    3. Add one line that prevents invented facts.
      Done when: the first response would need only light editing.
    Check your work
    Show model answer

    1. Missing context. Who the client is, the sale dates and offer, the audience (loyalty-app prospects), the goal (sign-ups), the brand rules (exact name, no competitors) and the length limit.

    2. Rewritten request. "You write social copy for FreshWay Market, a regional grocery chain. Audience: shoppers in San Diego County who do not yet use the FreshWay app. Task: draft five posts for the spring sale (20% off produce, 4 to 18 April) that drive downloads of the loyalty app. Constraints: write the name exactly as 'FreshWay Market'; never mention or compare with other grocers; under 200 characters each; friendly, not pushy. Format: a numbered list, each post followed by a suggested hashtag."

    3. Grounding line. "Use only the offer details above; if something you need is missing, say so instead of inventing it."
