    Protecting Sensitive Data

    Exam skillData Protection in Practice

    Applying data-protection practice to work involving sensitive, confidential, or regulated information, including classification, redaction and anonymization, upload judgment for code-executed analysis, and memory and incognito controls, consistent with organizational policy.

    Know the data's sensitivity before it enters any feature. Classify it, share only what the task needs, de-identify thoroughly (including free text and rare details), use only approved paths for regulated data, and use memory and incognito controls for what they actually do.

    Written and maintained by Prakash Sridharan, Claude Certified Architect

    On this pageWhat you need to knowIn the examCheck your understandingApply itSources

    What you need to know

    Most data leaks are routine decisions made without thinking: a spreadsheet uploaded as is, a personal account used because it was faster. Data protection is the habit of deciding before the upload.

    Classify first

    TierExamplesHandling
    Green: safe to usePublished material, anonymized aggregates, internal documents cleared for wide sharingNormal use
    Yellow: review firstInternal-only documents, names and contact details, unannounced dealsCheck policy; minimize; approved workspace
    Red: keep out unless an approved path existsHealth, financial and government-regulated data, credentials, third-party confidential materialConfirm an approved, compliant path first; otherwise keep it out and escalate

    When unsure between two tiers, treat the data as the more sensitive one until confirmed.

    Share only what the task needs

    Trends by region need no customer names; a summary for leadership needs no witness identities. Remove what the task does not depend on before upload. Asking Claude to ignore or anonymize data after upload still shares it.

    De-identify properly

    • Direct identifiers: names, IDs, account numbers, emails.
    • Quasi-identifiers: a unique job title plus a hire date, a rare condition, a small department's numbers.
    • Free text: comments, notes and tickets that say "This is Maria from Lakeside Dental."
    Exam trap
    Redaction that breaks the task

    Finding duplicate customer records needs the names and addresses. When the task depends on the identifiers, redaction is not the answer: use an approved path for that data, or keep it out.

    What the controls do

    ControlDoesDoes not
    Incognito chatKeeps the chat out of history and memoryMake restricted data safe to share; it is still retained under retention policy
    Memory controlsStop details persisting across chats; review and delete entriesChange what was already shared
    Code executionProcesses uploaded files in a sandboxDecide whether the file should have been uploaded
    Approved workspaceProvides the organization's agreed terms and controls, such as agreements needed for health dataRemove the need to minimize data
    Decision rule
    Allowed here, then how

    For regulated data, settle "is this workspace approved for this data?" before "how do I handle it here?"

    In the exam

    Items show a dataset, a policy and a proposed action. Pick the step that classifies first, minimizes and de-identifies before upload, uses the approved path, and sets memory to match sensitivity. Distractors rely on incognito, restricted sharing of identifiable data, anonymizing after upload, removing names only, or personal accounts.

    Check your understanding

    Question 1

    Policy allows AI analysis of de-identified data. An export includes names and emails the analysis does not need. First step?

    Show answer

    Answer: C. Strip identifiers the task does not need before sharing.

    Question 2

    Names were removed, but a unique job title and hire date remain. Problem?

    Show answer

    Answer: A. Unique combinations can re-identify individuals.

    Question 3

    A user thinks incognito makes it safe to paste card numbers. What is correct?

    Show answer

    Answer: D. Incognito controls history and memory, not permission.

    Apply it: Prepare Copperline's complaint analysis

    Exercise · about 20 minCase: Copperline Credit Union

    Compliance wants code-executed analysis of 18 months of member complaints to find fee-related patterns by branch. The export includes member names, account numbers, branch, complaint category, free-text descriptions (some mention medical bills and job loss) and dates.

    1. Classify each field.
    2. Prepare the data for upload.
    3. Choose the workspace and controls.
      Done when: the analysis is possible and nothing unnecessary is shared.
    Check your work
    Show model answer

    1. Account numbers: red (never in Claude, by policy). Names: yellow, not needed. Branch, category, dates: needed; low sensitivity in aggregate. Free text: yellow to red; contains identifying and sensitive details.

    2. Remove account numbers and names; replace each member with a code. Generalize dates to month. Screen the free text: remove names, employers, medical details and any amounts tied to a person, or replace the text with a category tag where detail is not needed. Check small branches for counts that could single out a member.

    3. The Enterprise workspace only, with memory off as policy requires for member work; code execution for the counts; results reviewed by compliance before they leave the team.
