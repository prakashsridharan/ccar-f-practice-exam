    Is This Use Case Appropriate?

    Exam skillUse-Case Appropriateness

    Evaluating a proposed Claude use case for appropriateness, including task suitability, delegation limits, trust in third-party Skills and connectors, and the Anthropic Usage Policy, and classifying it as appropriate, appropriate with safeguards, or inappropriate with documented rationale.

    Classify each proposed use of Claude as appropriate, appropriate with safeguards, or inappropriate, with a documented rationale. Use the delegation criteria and the Anthropic Usage Policy, define any safeguard as who reviews what and when, and check third-party Skills and connectors before enabling them.

    Written and maintained by Prakash Sridharan, Claude Certified Architect

    On this pageWhat you need to knowIn the examCheck your understandingApply itSources

    What you need to know

    Governance is a practitioner skill: the policy sets the boundary, and you decide, use case by use case, whether a proposal is appropriate. "It feels risky" is not a decision anyone can review.

    Three classifications

    ClassificationTypical profileExample
    AppropriateReversible, low consequence, grounded, normal reviewDrafting internal FAQ answers from approved policies
    Appropriate with safeguardsUseful, but stakes or accountability need a defined human gateDrafting replies to complaints, checked by an agent before sending
    InappropriateConsequence, irreversibility, need for human judgment, or the Usage Policy rules it outFinal unreviewed clinical, legal, eligibility or discipline decisions; fake reviews

    Screen with the delegation criteria (reversibility, consequence, need for human judgment or empathy, accountability) and the Anthropic Usage Policy, which sets out prohibited uses and extra requirements for some higher-risk uses, such as review by a qualified professional and telling consumers they are dealing with AI. Organizational policy can add stricter rules.

    Name the load-bearing criterion

    The criteria interact. A condolence message is low-stakes and reversible, yet a person should own it because empathy matters. A financial summary is consequential, yet appropriate when a named reviewer signs off before it is used. Name the criterion that decides the call and the human role that must own the work.

    Decision rule
    A safeguard is a defined gate

    State who reviews (the accountable role), what they check (the specific risk: accuracy, fairness, policy) and when (before the output is used). "We'll keep a human in the loop" is not a gate.

    Trust in Skills and connectors

    A Skill runs with whatever access the session already has; a connector reaches data and may act on it. Before enabling either, check:

    • Source: Anthropic-provided or organization-approved is the lower-risk start; an unknown publisher needs review. Internal does not mean vetted.
    • Reach: what it could read or do, and whether that is proportional to the task (least privilege).
    • Appropriateness: whether it is the right tool at all.

    Outcomes: enable, escalate to the admin or security team, or decline. A new version that reaches further needs a fresh check.

    Exam trap
    Disclaimers and consent as fixes

    A disclaimer, a notice or advance consent does not supply the judgment and accountability a consequential decision needs.

    In the exam

    Items describe a proposed use, a Skill or a connector and ask for the classification, the missing safeguard, or the right action. Weigh the criteria, check the Usage Policy, define gates in who-what-when form, and match access to purpose. Distractors approve because a better model is used, add disclaimers, trust internal origin or popularity, or trial risky access live.

    Check your understanding

    Question 1

    A proposal says Claude will screen grant applications "with a human in the loop." What makes it a defined gate?

    Show answer

    Answer: C. A gate names the accountable reviewer, the risk checked and the timing.

    Question 2

    A colleague found an unknown publisher's spreadsheet Skill; the account reaches finance files. Action?

    Show answer

    Answer: A. An unknown source with broad reach needs organizational review.

    Question 3

    A legal aid group wants final eviction-advice letters without attorney review. Classification?

    Show answer

    Answer: D. Consequential legal advice carries professional accountability.

    Apply it: Classify Fairhaven's proposals

    Exercise · about 20 minCase: Fairhaven Unified School District

    Proposals: (A) Claude drafts teachers' weekly class updates to parents from teachers' notes; (B) Claude ranks applicants for 30 teaching-assistant positions and rejects the bottom half automatically; (C) a teacher wants to enable a free third-party "grading helper" Skill from an unknown publisher; (D) Claude drafts special-education plan goals for the team to review and finalize.

    1. Classify each with the deciding criterion.
    2. Define the gate for each "with safeguards" case.
    3. Decide the Skill action.
      Done when: every decision could be explained to the superintendent.
    Check your work
    Show model answer

    A. Appropriate with safeguards. External but reversible before sending; gate: the teacher reads and approves each update, checking facts and tone, before it goes to parents.

    B. Inappropriate as proposed. Automatic rejections are consequential decisions about people with accountability (and fairness risk). An acceptable redesign: Claude summarizes applications against documented job criteria; HR reviews everyone, including those ranked lower, before any decision.

    C. Escalate. Unknown publisher, and grading touches student work. The district's technology office reviews source and reach; until then, do not enable.

    D. Appropriate with safeguards. Consequential but drafted for accountable specialists; gate: the special-education team reviews and finalizes every goal before the plan meeting, using de-identified inputs.
