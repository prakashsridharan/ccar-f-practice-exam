    Policy, Ethics and Diligence

    Exam skillPolicy, Ethics, and Diligence

    Analyzing observed usage patterns against organizational AI policies, including compliance gaps, ethical risks such as bias and transparency failures, Diligence lapses, and the distinction between a policy gap and a training gap, and determining the corrective action that closes the gap.

    Compare how people actually use Claude with what the AI policy requires. Diagnose each gap as a compliance breach, a policy gap or a training gap, recognize ethical risks such as bias and transparency failures, and choose the corrective action that closes the gap at its cause.

    Written and maintained by Prakash Sridharan, Claude Certified Architect

    On this pageWhat you need to knowIn the examCheck your understandingApply itSources

    What you need to know

    A policy that is followed only when someone is watching is not governance. Diligence in the AI Fluency framework is taking responsibility for AI-assisted work: using it ethically, being transparent about AI involvement, and verifying what you ship.

    Three kinds of gap

    GapWhat you seeCorrective action
    Policy gapThe policy is silent on a real practice (media replies, translation, how outputs are shared)Propose a policy update; put an interim control in place now
    Training gapA clear rule exists, but people do not know or understand it (new hires never shown it, an update never rolled out)Training and communication, and a check that it reached people
    Compliance breachA clear, known rule was not followed (review skipped, restricted data in a personal account)Contain the exposure, correct it, reinforce the rule; address what pushed people off the compliant path
    Example
    One audit, three diagnoses

    Layoff notices sent without the required HR review: breach. Customer letters translated with Claude, which the policy never mentions: policy gap. An analyst who never learned the memory rule for client work: training gap, after containing the exposure.

    Make the compliant path the default

    Diligence lapses often come from pressure or friction: reviews skipped on Friday afternoons, a slow approved workspace. A built-in gate, a confirmation step or adjusted deadlines change the pattern; a one-off reminder or blame does not.

    Ethical risks in ordinary outputs

    • Bias: patterns across outputs, such as women described by personality and men by achievement, smaller discounts in certain ZIP codes, or non-standard English summarized as "unclear." Look across the set, not one output; investigate even without explicit intent.
    • Transparency failures: required AI disclosure omitted, paraphrased text presented as direct quotes, invented testimonials.
    • Diligence lapses: sign-off without checking because "Claude is always right."

    When no rule settles it

    Reason it through: who is affected, what could go wrong, what a fair outcome requires, what disclosure the setting calls for. Document the reasoning and safeguards. When the stakes are large or the question is beyond your standing, escalate to the AI governance or ethics function with that reasoning, rather than reinterpreting the policy in your own favor.

    Exam trap
    Accountability moves to the tool

    "Claude wrote it" is never a defense. The person who ships the work owns it.

    In the exam

    Items describe observed usage and ask what kind of gap it is, which ethical risk it shows, or which corrective action fits. Diagnose first (silent policy, unknown rule, ignored rule), then fix at the cause. Distractors treat a policy gap as a training failure, discipline people for breaking unwritten rules, ban tools wholesale, or wait for complaints.

    Check your understanding

    Question 1

    New hires uploaded customer contracts to personal accounts; the clear policy was never in their onboarding. Which gap?

    Show answer

    Answer: A. The rule exists; people were not taught it.

    Question 2

    Staff use Claude for media replies; the policy says nothing about media. Some drafts skipped the press office. Best response?

    Show answer

    Answer: C. When the policy is silent, fix the policy and add an interim control.

    Question 3

    Scholarship announcements describe women by personality and men by achievement over a semester. Response?

    Show answer

    Answer: B. Patterns across outputs are bias risks that need correction and a control.

    Apply it: Audit Brightwater's quarter

    Exercise · about 20 minCase: Brightwater Communications

    A quarterly review of Claude use at Brightwater finds: (A) three deliverables for clients whose contracts require AI disclosure went out without it; (B) account executives paste client data into personal accounts on weekends because the Team workspace needs a VPN at home; (C) staff use Claude to write customer testimonials "inspired by" survey comments, posted as quotes; (D) the policy says nothing about AI-generated images in campaigns, which teams have started using.

    1. Diagnose each finding.
    2. Choose the corrective action for each.
    3. Say what you would escalate, and to whom.
      Done when: each action targets the cause, not just the symptom.
    Check your work
    Show model answer

    A. Compliance breach (or training gap if staff did not know the contract terms). Correct the three deliverables with the clients; add a disclosure checkbox to the delivery checklist for those accounts; brief account teams on which contracts require it.

    B. Compliance breach driven by friction. Contain: stop the practice and have the data removed from personal accounts where possible. Fix the cause: work with IT to make the approved workspace usable from home; remind staff of the rule.

    C. Transparency failure. Stop posting; replace with exact quotes the customers approve, or clearly labeled paraphrases.

    D. Policy gap. Propose a policy section on AI-generated images (disclosure, rights, review); interim rule: the account director approves any AI image before use.

    Escalate B (client data exposure) and C (misleading consumers) to the managing director, Elena Ruiz, and the agency's AI policy owner with the findings and actions.
