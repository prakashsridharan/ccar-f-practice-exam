    How to read a CCAR-P question

    Most items give a scenario, a constraint and four designs. Six habits pick the right one:

    1. Find the constraint that decides it. A latency target, an audit need, where data may be stored, a budget or an approved vendor usually rules out two options.
    2. Facts come from the system that owns them. Balances and status are fetched with a tool; numbers are computed in code. Claude does the language work.
    3. Enforce outside the model. Permissions, limits and approvals live in code and gates, never only in the prompt.
    4. Start simple; change on evidence. The simplest pattern, model and prompt that meet the bar, changed only when an evaluation shows a gap.
    5. Measure the right thing. The slowest requests under load, cost per successful outcome, and results per group of cases (a "slice"), not one average.
    6. Find the cause before the fix. A bigger model is rarely the first answer.

    Remember: turn wishes into measurable requirements, and write down why each decision was made.

    On exam day

    • 63 items in 120 minutes is just under two minutes each: flag anything slow. Read how many responses to select before the options.
    • Every "Not" above is a real trap. If an option sounds safe but does not touch the cause, it is probably wrong.
    • The score is scaled to 1,000 with 720 to pass, so a raw percentage does not convert directly. Aim for 80% or more in the practice tests.
