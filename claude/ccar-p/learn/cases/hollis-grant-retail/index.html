    Case study · fictional company

    Hollis & Grant Home

    An online home-goods retailer whose support centre handles 95,000 contacts a month, most of them repeat questions that depend on live order data.

    The business

    Hollis & Grant Home, based in Columbus, Ohio, sells furniture and homewares online in North America, about 2.1 million orders a year. Its support centre handles 95,000 contacts a month: 55% by chat and 45% by email. Volume rises to 2.5 times normal in November and December.

    What customers contact support about

    Contact typeShareWhere the answer lives
    Order status ("where is my order?")38%Order Management System, live
    Returns and refunds24%Returns portal and refund policy
    Product questions (assembly, care, dimensions)21%Product manuals and help centre
    Account and billing9%CRM and payment provider
    Complaints8%Needs a person

    Measured baseline

    • Average handling time: 7.5 minutes per chat, 11 minutes per email.
    • Customer satisfaction (CSAT): 78%. First-contact resolution: 64%.
    • 120 support agents at a loaded cost of $32 an hour.
    • Refunds over $250 need a supervisor's approval.

    Systems and content

    SystemRoleNotes
    Order Management SystemLive order and delivery statusAPI, 95th-percentile response 250 ms
    Returns portalCreates returns and refundsAPI; refund rules are configured here
    Product catalogue8,000 products, 2,300 PDF manualsManuals change when a product is revised
    Help centre400 articlesUpdated weekly by the support content team
    CRMCustomer history and contact recordsAgents work every contact here

    Constraints and targets

    • Chat: first response within 3 seconds at the 95th percentile. Email: reply within 4 hours.
    • Card numbers are handled by a PCI-certified payment provider and must never enter the model's context.
    • The approved run budget for AI is $18,000 a month, including peak season.
    • Implementation is estimated at $250,000 one-off, plus $6,000 a month for hosting, monitoring and support.

    Size of the inputs

    • System prompt, policies and tool definitions: a stable 6,000 tokens.
    • A typical chat runs 4 turns. Each turn adds about 2,500 tokens of conversation and retrieved or tool content, and produces about 300 tokens of output.

    People

    StakeholderCares most about
    Director of Customer Experience (sponsor)CSAT, resolution, peak-season coverage
    Head of E-commerce EngineeringIntegration effort, reliability, on-call load
    Finance business partnerRun cost, payback, headcount assumptions
    Support team leadsEscalation quality, agent workload
