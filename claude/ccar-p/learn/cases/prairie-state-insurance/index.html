Case study · fictional company

Prairie State Mutual

A property and auto insurer whose claims intake team keys every new claim by hand, misses its acknowledgement SLA and makes costly keying errors.

The business

Prairie State Mutual, based in Des Moines, Iowa, is a mid-sized property and auto insurer with customers in the United States and the European Union. It receives about 18,000 new claims a month (first notice of loss).

How intake works today

ChannelShareWhat arrives
Email60%A free-text description, often with photos and attachments
Web form30%Structured fields plus uploaded photos and documents
Scanned mail10%Handwritten or printed forms, sometimes with medical reports

Fourteen intake handlers read each claim, key 31 fields into ClaimsCore (the claims system of record), check that the policy was active on the date of loss, request any missing documents, and send an acknowledgement to the customer.

Measured baseline (last quarter's operations data):

  • Average handling time: 22 minutes per claim.
  • Keying errors: 6% of claims have at least one wrong field (from a monthly quality sample of 400 claims). Each error takes 35 minutes of rework later.
  • Acknowledgement SLA (within one business day): met 81% of the time. Target: 98%.
  • Loaded handler cost: $48 an hour.

What Prairie State is considering next

  • Fast-track for small auto glass claims. About 2,400 windshield claims a month, averaging $420, almost never disputed. Claims operations wants them paid within a day when the policy covers glass, instead of the current five days.
  • Claim prioritization. A queue score so that the most urgent claims (injuries, uninhabitable homes) reach adjusters first.

Systems

SystemRoleNotes
ClaimsCoreSystem of record for claimsREST API for creating and updating claims
Policy AdminPolicy status, cover and limitsAPI, 95th-percentile response 300 ms
Fraud rules engineScores each claim against fixed rulesOwned by the Special Investigations team
Document storeHolds every attachmentRetains documents for 7 years

Data and regulation

  • Claims contain personal data; injury claims also contain health information from medical reports.
  • EU claimants are covered by GDPR; their data must be processed in the EU. US customers' data stays in the US.
  • The regulator does not allow automated claim denials: a licensed adjuster makes every denial decision. Payouts over $10,000 need a senior adjuster's approval.

Size of the inputs

  • A typical claim package is about 9,000 tokens; the 95th percentile is 40,000 tokens (long medical reports). 95% of packages are under 20 pages; the longest run to about 120 pages.
  • The extraction instructions and the 31-field schema add a stable 4,000 tokens to every request.
  • A drafted acknowledgement is about 1,200 tokens of output including the extracted fields.
