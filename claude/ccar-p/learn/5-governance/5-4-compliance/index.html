Regulatory Compliance

Exam taskEnsure compliance with regulations (e.g., GDPR, HIPAA, FedRAMP)

Turn regulatory obligations such as HIPAA, GDPR, data residency and FedRAMP into a control register where every obligation has a technical control, an accountable owner and evidence that it works.

On this pageWhat you need to knowIn the examCheck your understandingApply itSources

What you need to know

Compliance for AI systems is not a statement in a design document. It is a set of controls that can be shown to work. The exam expects you to know the main regimes at an architect's level and, above all, to connect each obligation to something enforceable and provable.

The regimes you will meet

RegimeApplies toWhat it means for the architecture
HIPAAUS protected health information handled for covered entitiesA Business Associate Agreement must cover the specific service and configuration; features outside that coverage (often beta features) cannot touch PHI
GDPRPersonal data of people in the EUA lawful basis, data minimization, a data processing agreement, rights such as access and erasure, and lawful mechanisms for transfers outside the EU
Data residencyContractual, regulatory or internal rules on where data is processed and storedThe model's processing location and every copy: logs, caches, traces, backups, vector stores
FedRAMPUS federal cloud workloadsOnly routes authorized at the required impact level qualify
Sector and internal rulesFinancial, legal, public sector, company policyApproved vendors and routes, retention periods, privilege and audit requirements

Note that GDPR does not by itself demand that data stay in the EU; an explicit residency requirement does. Read the requirement in the scenario carefully.

The control register

For every obligation, record four things:

Decision rule
A control without evidence is an assertion

"We comply with HIPAA" is not a control. A gateway allowlist that blocks non-covered features, owned by a named person, with a configuration export from last quarter, is.

Keep it current

Configurations drift, vendors add features, new routes appear, and evidence goes stale. Re-validate the register on a schedule and after every significant change (a new model, a new feature, a new data source).

Constraints come first

Check compliance before choosing the integration route. A route that fails a regulatory or procurement requirement is ruled out regardless of its technical merits (lesson 3.1).

Example
Residency beyond the model call

A European insurer configured its model calls to run in an EU region and declared residency met. An audit found that the observability vendor stored full request traces in the US, and the vector database's backups replicated to another continent. The fix was a data-flow record listing every copy of claim data, with each location configured and evidenced.

Exam trap
"The provider is certified, so we are compliant"

A provider's certification or a general agreement does not make every configuration compliant. The specific route, features and data flows must be covered.

Exam trap
Residency checked only at inference

Logs, caches, traces, backups and embeddings are copies of the data. A residency control that covers only the model call is incomplete.

In the exam

Compliance questions describe a regulated workload and offer designs or evidence. Strong answers select a route covered for the exact configuration, account for every copy of the data, and map obligations to controls, owners and evidence. Distractors rely on prompt instructions ("handle PHI carefully"), general certifications, or documentation without proof.

Check your understanding

Question 1

A healthcare startup has a BAA with its AI provider and wants to use a new beta feature on patient notes. What must be checked first?

Show answer

Answer: B. BAA coverage is specific to services and features. A beta feature outside that coverage cannot process PHI, however useful it is.

Question 2

Which is acceptable evidence that decisions can be explained to a regulator?

Show answer

Answer: A. Evidence must show the control working. Reconstructing an actual decision proves the logs contain what an explanation needs.

Question 3 · select 2

A contract requires that customer data never leave Canada. Which TWO items must the design cover besides the model call?

Show answer

Answer: A, B. Every copy of customer data counts toward residency, including observability data, embeddings, caches and backups.

Apply it: Build Lakeview's control register

Exercise · about 20 minCase: Lakeview Regional Medical Center

Build the control register for Lakeview's discharge-summary drafter, using the case facts.

  1. List the obligations: at least five, from HIPAA, residency, audit, retention and the hospital's own rules.
  2. For each, name the control, the owner and the evidence.
    Done when: every evidence item is something you could hand to an auditor.
  3. Map every copy of PHI in the system, so residency can be shown.
  4. Set the re-validation schedule and the events that trigger an early check.

3. Copies of PHI. EHR (source); the drafting service's request and response logs; traces in the observability tool; the evaluation dataset (de-identified, in the test environment); temporary storage of day summaries; backups of each. Every one is in the US region, and the observability tool is configured to exclude message content.

4. Re-validation. Quarterly, plus early checks after any new model or feature, a new data source, a change of observability vendor, or a change to the BAA.
