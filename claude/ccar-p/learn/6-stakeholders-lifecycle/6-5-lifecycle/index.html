    Supporting the Lifecycle

    Exam taskSupport lifecycle phases (discovery, design, handoff, monitoring, iteration)

    Carry requirements through discovery, design, handoff, monitoring and iteration, decide when to iterate and when to re-architect, and gate expansion on documented business outcomes.

    On this pageWhat you need to knowIn the examCheck your understandingApply itSources

    What you need to know

    An AI solution is not delivered once. It moves through phases, and the architect's job is to make sure what was learned in one phase shapes the next.

    The five phases

    PhaseMain workKey outputs
    DiscoveryUnderstand goals; translate them into requirements and assumptionsTranslation table, requirements, assumption register, success criteria
    DesignChoose patterns, models, routes; weigh trade-offsArchitecture, decision log, evaluation plan, control register
    HandoffTransfer the design and its reasons to builders and operatorsImplementation guidance, runbooks, evaluation set
    MonitoringWatch signals, SLAs and governance checkpointsDashboards, governance table, reviews
    IterationImprove, fix, extend, or re-architectChanges gated by evaluation; updated documents; outcome measurement

    The phases connect. A residency requirement found in discovery leads to a regional route in design, a recorded rejection of the global endpoint at handoff, a scheduled residency check in monitoring, and constraints on any performance fix during iteration.

    Iterate or re-architect?

    SituationResponse
    Inconsistent output formatIterate on the prompt
    Retrieval quality slippingTune chunking, filters or index
    Cost over budget from bloated contextOptimize context and caching
    Small latency variation within SLAMonitor
    A new mandatory approval step changes the workflowRe-architect that workflow
    The chosen route cannot meet a new residency obligationRevisit the deployment architecture
    A model version is being retiredPlan a migration, re-run the evaluation suite, update prompts if needed

    Diagnose first (lesson 4.4); re-architect only when the cause is structural.

    The outcome document

    Technical success is not business success. Before expanding a deployment, document the outcome:

    FieldCaptures
    Use caseThe business problem and its scope boundary
    Metric beforeThe baseline, measured before deployment
    Metric afterThe same metric, measured the same way, after a long enough period
    Auditable controlThe evidence that makes the result trustworthy (for example, approval logs)
    Measurement ownerThe named person responsible for the measurement
    Reuse potentialWhere the solution or its patterns could apply next
    Decision rule
    Expansion waits for evidence

    If the "after" measurement period is too short, do not extrapolate. Confirm the control is working, name the owner, set the date when the measurement will be complete, and hold the expansion decision until then.

    Model and platform lifecycle

    Models are versioned and eventually retired. Pin versions in production, track deprecation notices, and keep the evaluation suite ready so a migration is a planned release, not an emergency. The regression suite is what makes this routine.

    Example
    A pilot that was not ready to scale

    After four weeks, a sales-proposal assistant had excellent technical metrics, and the sales director wanted to roll it out to all regions. The architect's outcome document showed "time to first draft" had only two weeks of clean data, and win-rate impact (the real goal) would take a quarter to observe. The rollout was approved for one more region, with the full decision scheduled for the end of the quarter.

    Exam trap
    Technical metrics as proof of value

    Latency, availability and evaluation scores show the system works. They do not show it delivered the business outcome. Expansion questions want the outcome document.

    Exam trap
    Re-architecting for a prompt problem

    A wording or format issue is fixed by iteration. Options that rebuild the architecture for it are disproportionate.

    In the exam

    Lifecycle questions ask which phase an activity belongs to, whether a problem calls for iteration or re-architecture, or whether a deployment is ready to expand. Carry constraints through every phase, diagnose before re-architecting, and gate expansion on an outcome document with a real before-and-after measurement, an auditable control and a named owner.

    Check your understanding

    Question 1

    Four weeks after launch, a sponsor wants to expand a deployment. The "after" metric has two weeks of data and the control logs have not been checked. What should the architect do?

    Show answer

    Answer: B. Expansion is gated on evidence. Verify the control, assign ownership and complete the measurement before deciding, without inventing results or abandoning the work.

    Question 2

    A new regulation requires a person to approve every outbound letter the system sends, which it currently sends automatically. What is the right response?

    Show answer

    Answer: A. A new mandatory approval changes the workflow itself. That is structural, so it calls for re-architecting that part of the system, not iteration.

    Question 3 · select 2

    Which TWO belong in an outcome document?

    Show answer

    Answer: B, C. An outcome document ties business metrics before and after to an owner and an auditable control. Token counts and benchmarks are not business outcomes.

    Apply it: Write Lakeview's outcome document

    Exercise · about 20 minCase: Lakeview Regional Medical Center

    Lakeview piloted the discharge-summary drafter on two medical units for eight weeks. The CMIO wants to roll it out hospital-wide next month. Pilot data: average time per signed summary fell from 35 to 14 minutes on the pilot units (six weeks of clean data); coding returns on pilot summaries fell from 12% to 5% (four weeks of data, because coding lags); 98% of drafts were signed with minor or no edits; no medication discrepancies were found.

    1. Write the outcome document with all six fields.
    2. Decide what can be approved now, and what must wait.
      Done when: nothing you approve depends on extrapolated data.
    3. List what must be true before each new unit goes live, carrying constraints from earlier phases.
    4. Name one event that would trigger re-architecture rather than iteration.
    Check your work
    Show model answer

    1. Outcome document

    FieldContent
    Use caseDrafting discharge summaries from EHR notes on two medical units, with physician sign-off; out of scope: surgical, paediatric and psychiatric discharges
    Metric before35 minutes per signed summary; 12% coding returns
    Metric after14 minutes per signed summary (six weeks); 5% coding returns (four weeks, still maturing)
    Auditable controlEHR signature log linking every sent summary to a signing physician and a timestamp; medication check logs
    Measurement ownerClinical operations lead (time); coding team lead (returns)
    Reuse potentialOther medical units first; patterns (source-linked drafts, code-inserted medications) for transfer letters and clinic letters

    2. Decision. Approve expansion to the other medical units, where the case mix matches the pilot and the time saving is well evidenced. Hold surgical, paediatric and psychiatric units: their documentation differs and was not in the pilot or the evaluation set. Report coding returns again after eight weeks of data before claiming the billing benefit.

    3. Before each new unit

    • The unit's note types are in the evaluation set, and the release thresholds from lesson 4.2 are met for that slice.
    • The BAA-covered route and US-only data flows are unchanged (control register re-checked).
    • Physicians on the unit are trained on the review screen, and the signature control is confirmed working for the unit.
    • The unit's outcome baseline (minutes per summary, coding returns) is measured before go-live.

    4. Re-architecture trigger. A requirement that summaries be sent to primary care within an hour of discharge, which would need a different drafting and review flow, or a change of cloud provider that the current BAA does not cover.
