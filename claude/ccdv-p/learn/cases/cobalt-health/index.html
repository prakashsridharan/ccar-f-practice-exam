    CCDV-P/Learning guide/Case studies/Cobalt Health Network
    Case study · fictional company

    Cobalt Health Network

    A telehealth provider whose patient-message triage agent and visit-summary service run under a business associate agreement, with zero data retention and strict limits on what may be logged.

    The business

    Cobalt Health Network, based in Portland, Oregon, runs virtual primary care for 1.4 million members in six states. Patients message their care team through an app, and clinicians run video visits that are transcribed.

    Systems in production

    SystemWhat it doesVolume
    Message triage agentReads each patient message, classifies urgency, drafts a reply for a nurse, and books follow-ups through a scheduling tool1.1 million messages a month
    Visit-summary serviceTurns a visit transcript into a structured summary and a patient letter140,000 visits a month
    Clinical knowledge searchRetrieval over 3,200 care protocols and medication guidesCalled by both systems

    The triage agent runs on a self-hosted Agent SDK loop in Cobalt's own cloud account. Its tools are lookup_patient (demographics and allergies), get_care_plan, search_protocols, propose_appointment and draft_reply. Nurses approve every reply before it is sent.

    Measured baseline

    • Triage accuracy on urgent messages (nurse-confirmed): 96.5%. Target: 99% recall on urgent messages.
    • Median time from message to nurse-approved reply: 3.2 hours.
    • Visit summaries returned by clinicians for correction: 7%.
    • Monthly Claude spend: $58,000, of which the visit-summary service is 62%.

    Constraints

    • All patient data is covered by a business associate agreement and zero data retention. Cobalt's own policy forbids storing message or transcript content in observability systems; engineers may see content only for a consented debugging case, for at most 14 days.
    • An urgent message (chest pain, suicidal thoughts, stroke signs) must reach a nurse within 15 minutes.
    • The agent may never send a message to a patient or change a prescription.

    Recent events

    • A protocol update added a paragraph about protecting mental-health information. Afterwards the triage agent declined to classify 4% of messages about anxiety medication.
    • During a provider capacity incident last quarter, the summary service retried every failed call immediately and was rate-limited for 40 minutes after recovery.

    People

    StakeholderCares most about
    Chief Medical OfficerNo urgent message missed; clinicians trust the drafts
    Director of Platform EngineeringOn-call load, reliable releases
    Privacy OfficerNothing clinical in logs; audit evidence for regulators
    Finance leadSpend predictable within $65,000 a month
